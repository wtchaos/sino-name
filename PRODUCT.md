# Product

Brand name: **SinoName**. Confirm domain, search-conflict, and trademark availability before launch.

## Register

product

## Users

The primary users are English-speaking people outside China who are curious about Chinese culture and want a Chinese name that feels personally meaningful. They are exploring identity and culture rather than seeking professional naming, religious instruction, or fortune-telling. Most will use the product once, on mobile or desktop, and should be able to reach a complete result without creating an account.

## Product Purpose

The product gives each visitor one natural, credible Chinese name and explains it in clear English through linguistic meaning, poetic imagery, and optional Daoist and Buddhist perspectives. The name must sound like a name a Chinese speaker could genuinely use, not a literal transliteration or a collection of decorative characters.

The generated surname should, where possible, create a gentle connection to the user's family name through sound or meaning. This connection is optional: a common, harmonious Chinese surname always takes priority over forced transliteration.

The experience is English-first. Chinese appears where it is essential: the generated name, individual characters, pinyin, pronunciation guidance, and accurately attributed source text. Date of birth is required and time of birth is optional. Birth information may inspire seasonal, zodiac, time-of-day, and five-element symbolism, but it must never be presented as a precise fate calculation or prediction.

Poetic interpretation is included by default. Daoist and Buddhist perspectives are optional, off by default, and shown only when the user actively selects them.

The initial experience minimizes typing. First name and family name are the only expected free-text fields; gender expression, birth information, desired qualities, naming style, and cultural interpretation layers use constrained selections. The first anonymous session provides one complete name without requiring login.

For the MVP, the result includes a free name card that the user can save or share. Payments and paid-name unlocks are deferred. The result remains available in the same browser for 30 days. The service must not retain the user's English name or complete birth date long-term; persistence is limited to an anonymous visitor identifier, the generated result, and a server-keyed cache identifier that cannot be used as a plain lookup of the original profile.

Success means users receive a name they would be comfortable sharing or using, understand why it suits them, learn something accurate about Chinese language and culture, and never feel that they have visited a fortune-telling site.

## Brand Personality

Poetic, trustworthy, and culturally respectful. The voice should feel like a knowledgeable cultural guide: warm and evocative without becoming mystical, authoritative without becoming academic, and accessible without flattening Chinese traditions into novelty.

## Anti-references

- Fortune-telling, horoscope, destiny-prediction, or supernatural certainty.
- Cheap AI generators that present arbitrary outputs as personalized insight.
- Tourism-souvenir versions of Chinese culture: decorative clichés, exoticism, faux calligraphy, indiscriminate red-and-gold styling, dragons, lanterns, or temple imagery used as surface decoration.
- Game-character or fantasy-name generators.
- Literal phonetic transliteration that produces an unnatural Chinese name.
- Interfaces that hide most of the first result behind login walls, aggressive upsells, or casino-like reveals.

## Design Principles

1. **Natural name first.** Linguistic credibility, pronunciation, contemporary usage, and avoidance of harmful homophones take priority over poetic cleverness or similarity to the user's original name.
2. **Guide instead of mystify.** Explain cultural ideas clearly, distinguish verified sources from original interpretation, and never turn symbolism into a claim about destiny.
3. **Earn trust through specificity.** Show how each character contributes to the name, why it suits the selected preferences, and where an interpretation comes from.
4. **Ask less, personalize more.** Use short, selection-led progressive steps and reveal optional questions only when they materially improve the result.
5. **Protect the moment of discovery.** Let anonymous visitors receive one complete, useful name with minimal friction; security controls should remain invisible to legitimate users whenever possible.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All flows must support keyboard navigation, visible focus states, screen readers, sufficient color contrast, zoom and responsive reflow, reduced-motion preferences, and instructions that do not depend on color alone. Chinese characters, pinyin, tone marks, and pronunciation guidance must remain selectable and readable. Gender choices must include feminine, masculine, gender-neutral, and no-preference options without inferring identity from a person's English name. Cultural and religious explanations must be presented as interpretive perspectives rather than universal beliefs.
